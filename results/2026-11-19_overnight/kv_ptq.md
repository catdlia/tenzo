## Conclusion

- **No scheme reaches dPPL <= 5% at <= 2.5 bits/element**, on any scale of the study.
  - The best pure 2.5-bit scheme is S3 (ternary g=32 along D + randomized Hadamard) at +23% (4 EN windows).
  - With 4 INT8 sinks + 32 recent tokens in INT8 (S3+s4r32: 2.9 b/elem at ctx 512, asymptotically 2.5) it is +7.1% EN [5.5%, 8.8%] and +16.1% UK on the full window sets.
- **What helped:**
  - The rotation: S2 -> S3 is +44% -> +23% at the same bits.
  - INT8 sink/recent windows: +23% -> +9.9% (r32) -> +5.9% (r128, 4 windows).
- **What did not help:**
  - KIVI-style per-channel K over 32-token groups (S4, +31%) was worse than the rotation.
  - Per-token TWN (S1, +91%) is far worse than group-wise.
- **K is more sensitive than V:** K INT8 + ternary V gives +4.3% (4 EN windows), ternary K + V INT8 gives +10.9%.
- **Ukrainian degrades much more than English.** S3+s4r32: +16% UK vs +7% EN. K8V:S3: +36.5% UK vs +5.6% EN; its ternary V is worse on UK than the windowed scheme.
- **Simulator validity.** The engine replicas reproduce the engine within CI: tl1 56.14 vs 54.94, paired ratio 1.022 [0.996, 1.047]. Engine tl1_fused itself is x2.88 (EN) / x8.87 (UK).
- **Engine cost (not implemented).** The rotation in an engine needs an explicit FWHT per head on K, Q and V-out (O(D log D)); it cannot be folded into the ternary o_proj weights.
- **Point of comparison.** A 2-bit asymmetric g=32 + rotation comparator costs 3.0 b/elem at +12.9%, i.e. it is no better than S3+s4r32 at 2.9.
